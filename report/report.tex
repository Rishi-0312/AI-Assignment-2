\begin{abstract}
Wakes of upstream turbines cut the output of downstream machines by up to 75\% in the farm we study.
\emph{Wake steering} yaws upstream rotors away from the wind to deflect their wakes, but the industrial
look-up table (LUT) of set-points, optimised offline with an engineering model, ignores sensor noise,
yaw-drive limits and actuator wear. We cast farm-level yaw control as a Markov decision process with
stochastic wind, noisy sensing, rate-limited yaw drives and a wear penalty, simulated with NREL's FLORIS, and
solve it with PPO (implemented from scratch) using an exactly rotation-equivariant policy. On \NumEpisodes{}
held-out one-hour episodes of a 3$\times$3 farm the agent raises energy yield by \GainPPO\% over greedy
control, versus \GainLUT\% for a LUT optimised with the very wake model it is tested in and \GainLUTf\% for
that LUT with tuned measurement filtering, while yawing \TravelCutVsLUT\% less than the LUT.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large wind farms lose 10--20\% of their energy to wakes~\cite{barthelmie2009}. Yawing an upstream turbine by $\gamma$ costs it roughly a
$\cos^{1.88}\gamma$ share of its power but bends its wake off the downstream rotors, which can recover far
more~\cite{bastankhah2016,howland2019,fleming2019}. Choosing the offsets of all turbines is a
\emph{sequential} problem: the wind drifts and is measured noisily, yaw drives are slow and every degree of
travel wears them. Offline LUTs~\cite{fleming2022sr} treat each instant in isolation and trust perfect
models and measurements; RL optimises closed-loop behaviour from interaction~\cite{stanfel2020,dong2021,kadoche2023}.
\emph{Contributions:} (i) an MDP of dynamic wake steering with realistic sensing and actuation; (ii) a batched,
Gymnasium-compatible FLORIS environment fast enough for on-policy RL on a laptop; (iii) PPO with a policy that
is exactly equivariant to the farm's 90$^\circ$ rotation symmetry; (iv) a paired, held-out evaluation against
greedy and model-based control with ablations and robustness tests.

\section{MDP Formulation}
\textbf{Simulator.} Nine NREL 5-MW turbines~\cite{jonkman2009} ($D=126$\,m) on a 3$\times$3 grid with $5D$
spacing, turbulence intensity (TI) 6\%. Powers come from FLORIS~v4~\cite{floris} with the Gauss--Curl-Hybrid
model (Gaussian deficit, wake deflection, secondary steering, yaw-added recovery~\cite{king2021}). Step
$\Delta t=60$\,s; episode $T=60$ steps (one hour).

\textbf{Exogenous wind.} Direction: persistent random walk $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\delta_{t+1}$,
$\delta_{t+1}=0.7\,\delta_t+0.8^\circ\epsilon_t$, $\theta_0\sim\mathcal U[0^\circ,360^\circ)$. Speed:
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck $U_{t+1}=U_t+0.1(\bar U-U_t)+0.25\,\epsilon'_t$, $\bar U\sim\mathcal U[5,12]$\,m/s.
Controllers only see $\hat\theta_t=\theta_t+\mathcal N(0,2^{\circ\,2})$ and $\hat U_t=U_t+\mathcal N(0,0.2^2)$.

\textbf{State space $\mathcal S$.} The Markov state holds the true wind $(\theta,\delta,U,\bar U)$, the
measurements and the nacelle headings $h_i$. The agent observes $o_t\in\mathbb R^{22}$ (a POMDP):
\begin{equation*}
o_t=\Big[\sin\hat\theta_t,\ \cos\hat\theta_t,\ \tfrac{\hat U_t-8.5}{3.5},\ \tfrac{\Delta\hat\theta_t}{5^\circ},\
\big\{\tfrac{\hat\gamma_{i,t}}{25^\circ}\big\}_{i=1}^{9},\ \big\{\tfrac{P_{i,t}}{P_\mathrm{rated}}\big\}_{i=1}^{9}\Big],
\end{equation*}
with measured yaw offsets $\hat\gamma_i=\wrap(\hat\theta-h_i)$ and turbine outputs $P_i$, which depend on
the \emph{true} wind and thus act as a noise-free wake sensor.

\textbf{Action space $\mathcal A$.} Continuous $a_t\in[-1,1]^9$, yaw-offset set-points $\gamma^\star_i=25^\circ a_i$.
Each yaw drive turns at most $\omega=0.3^\circ$/s: $h_{i,t+1}=h_{i,t}+\Delta h_{i,t}$ with
$\Delta h_{i,t}=\mathrm{clip}\big(\wrap(\hat\theta_t-\gamma^\star_i-h_{i,t}),\pm\omega\Delta t\big)$. Then the
wind evolves and FLORIS returns $P_{i,t+1}$ for the true misalignments $\gamma_i=\theta_{t+1}-h_{i,t+1}$.

\textbf{Reward $\mathcal R$} (in \% of farm capacity $P_\mathrm{cap}=45$\,MW):
\begin{equation}
r_t=\tfrac{100}{P_\mathrm{cap}}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i}P_{i,t+1}-P^\mathrm{greedy}(\theta_{t+1},U_{t+1})\Big)-c\sum_i|\Delta h_{i,t}|.
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
$P^\mathrm{greedy}$ is the all-aligned farm's power in the same wind (tabulated). No controller can affect the
wind, so it shifts every policy's return equally: the optimal policy is unchanged while the variance due to
the $U^3$ power law vanishes. The term with $c=0.005$/deg prices yaw-drive wear and discourages chattering.

\section{Environment, Algorithm and Baselines}
\textbf{Environment.} A FLORIS call has $\approx$20\,ms fixed overhead, so 256 environments advance in
lock-step with \emph{one} vectorised FLORIS evaluation per step, split over worker processes (3.8k
steps/s on an i7-12700H laptop); a Gymnasium interface (\texttt{WakeSteer-v0}) is also provided.

\textbf{Symmetry-aware policy.} The grid is invariant under 90$^\circ$ rotation and FLORIS works in the wind
frame, so $P_i(\theta,\boldsymbol\gamma)=P_{\pi(i)}(\theta-90^\circ,\Pi\boldsymbol\gamma)$ for a turbine
permutation $\pi$ (verified to $10^{-6}$). Each observation is rotated by $k$ quarter turns into the sector
$\hat\theta-90^\circ k\in[15^\circ,105^\circ)$ (cut where steering gains vanish), turbine features are
permuted by $\pi^k$, and actions are permuted back: the policy is equivariant by construction and learns
only a quarter of the wind rose.

\textbf{PPO~\cite{schulman2017}.} Gaussian policy, separate 2$\times$256 tanh actor and critic, Fourier
features $\sin k\theta,\cos k\theta$ ($k\in\{1,2,3,4,8\}$). Clipped surrogate ($\epsilon=0.2$),
GAE~\cite{schulman2016} ($\lambda=0.9$), 256 envs$\times$30 steps per update, 10 epochs$\times$8 minibatches,
Adam $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$ (annealed), KL early stop at 0.02, per-network gradient clipping, bootstrapping at
time-limit truncations, random initial offsets; $3\!\times\!10^6$ steps per run ($\approx$1\,h); the
checkpoint with the best return on fixed validation weather is kept. The discount $\gamma$ is a training
hyper-parameter (the objective is undiscounted energy), selected from $\{0,0.5,0.8,0.95\}$ on validation
weather. PPO fits: actions are continuous and 9-D (a discretised DQN would face $\gg10^8$ joint actions)
and simulated experience is cheap.

\textbf{Baselines.} \emph{Greedy}: zero offsets (industry standard). \emph{LUT}: offsets optimised offline
with FLORIS' Serial-Refine method~\cite{fleming2022sr} on a $1^\circ\times1$\,m/s grid (3\,960 optimisations)
using the \emph{same} wake model as the test, applied at the measured wind---a model-based reference with
perfect model knowledge. \emph{LUT+filter}: the same LUT fed with exponentially smoothed measurements, the
nacelles tracking the smoothed direction (smoothing factor $\alpha=\AlphaLUTf$ tuned on validation weather).
\emph{Random} set-points. All share the yaw dynamics and are tested on \NumEpisodes{} one-hour episodes of
unseen weather, every controller facing \emph{identical} wind (paired comparison).

\textbf{Learning and ablations.} \emph{Discount:} the best validation rewards were \ValRewardZero,
\ValRewardHalf, \ValRewardPointEight{} and \ValRewardNinetyFive{} for $\gamma=0$, 0.5, 0.8 and 0.95
(Fig.~\ref{fig:training}b). Long-horizon returns are dominated by the exogenous wind, whereas a yaw drive
reaches any set-point within 1--3 steps; a two-step horizon ($\gamma=0.5$) captures the cost of multi-step
yaw manoeuvres at low variance, and even the myopic agent is close. \emph{Symmetry} (controlled at
$\gamma=0.95$, Fig.~\ref{fig:training}a): the C$_4$-equivariant policy learns about 3$\times$ faster and
plateaus higher than a plain MLP (test gain \AblNoSymmetryGain\% vs \AblLongHorizonGain\%).
\emph{Inputs and reward:} without turbine-power inputs the gain drops to \AblNoPowersGain\%; without the wear
penalty yaw travel rises to \AblNoYawCostTravel$^\circ$/h (gain \AblNoYawCostGain\%).

\section{Conclusion}
PPO trained in FLORIS learns wake steering that outperforms greedy control and model-optimised LUTs (raw and
filtered) under realistic sensing and actuation, with less yaw activity and \DecisionPPO\,ms decisions
(online FLORIS optimisation: \DecisionSR\,ms). Encoding the farm's rotation symmetry and a short training
horizon were key to sample efficiency. Limitations: steady-state wakes (no advection delay), one layout,
simulated sensors.
